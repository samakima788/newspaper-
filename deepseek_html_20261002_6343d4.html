Parliament passes landmark data protection bill after marathon session

The new law establishes an independent data protection authority and introduces strict penalties for misuse of personal information.

By Rahim Ahmed · Published: October 3, 2026, 10:42 PM · Updated: 11:15 PM · 6 min read

The Parliament building in Dhaka. Photo: Demo / NEWSROOM

DHAKA — Parliament on Thursday passed the long-awaited Data Protection Bill 2026 after a marathon session that stretched past midnight, establishing the country's first independent data protection authority and introducing strict penalties for the misuse of personal information.

The bill, which had been under discussion for over two years, passed with 241 votes in favor and 87 against. It creates a seven-member Data Protection Commission empowered to investigate complaints, levy fines of up to 5% of annual global turnover, and order the deletion of unlawfully processed data.

"This is a significant step forward for digital rights in our country," said the minister presenting the bill. "It balances the need for innovation with the fundamental right to privacy."

Opposition members, while supporting the general thrust of the legislation, raised concerns about the commission's independence and the scope of exemptions for government agencies. An amendment to narrow those exemptions was defeated by a vote of 198 to 112.

The law is expected to come into force within six months, with a transition period for businesses to comply. Industry groups have welcomed the clarity but warned that smaller enterprises may struggle with the compliance burden.

"This law will shape how millions of people interact with technology for years to come."

— Digital rights advocate, speaking to NEWSROOM
